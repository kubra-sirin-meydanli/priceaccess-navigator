Scope

SyntaRelX is a fictional IL-17A biologic, built to resemble a mature specialty product with an established European footprint. It does not exist. Every price, volume and margin figure here is invented to exercise the model — no conclusion transfers to any real asset.

6 modelled marketsReference markets held constant3-year horizonEx-factory list price

The engine works on ex-factory list prices — the prices reference baskets actually observe. Confidential net prices (Erstattungsbetrag arrangements, managed entry agreements, rebates) are invisible to referencing countries and play no part in the cascade. They enter only at the impact layer, through a gross-to-net assumption.

The IRP engine

Under International Reference Pricing a country sets its price against a basket of others. Change one price, and every market referencing it recalculates. The engine models that propagation — but what generates movement here is a transmission assumption, not the reference rule alone. That distinction matters more than anything else on this page, so it comes first.

The load-bearing assumption: marginal transmission

Every market in this dataset is priced above its basket minimum — France sits at €620 against a floor of €560, Türkiye at €195 against a floor of €310. That is what real markets look like: a reference price is one input to a negotiation, not a formula that fires on its own. A strict ceiling reading, where a price only moves once the cap falls beneath it, would therefore produce no cascade at all.

The engine instead applies marginal transmission: when a market's reference floor falls by €X, its price falls by €X, whether or not the floor was binding. The historical gap above the floor is preserved; only the movement travels. This is a behavioural claim about how regulators and manufacturers renegotiate against a lowered anchor — not a mechanical property of IRP, and the assumption most worth arguing with.

Propagation

Iterates to a fixed point. France references the Netherlands and the Netherlands references France — a real cycle. The engine recalculates until nothing moves. Each price is derived from baseline values rather than current ones, so the system is monotone, floored at zero, and converges to a unique solution. It cannot ratchet.

Free markets never receive. Germany and the UK reference no one, so nothing propagates into them. Both still sit in other markets' baskets, so they can transmit.

Reference markets are held constant. Countries appearing in baskets but outside the six — Italy, Belgium, Portugal, Greece, Norway — supply prices and never update. A scope decision with real consequences: see the limitations.

Rules live in data, not code. Baskets and calculation methods sit in baskets.yaml, so they can be reviewed and corrected by a domain expert without touching Python. That matters — this model's France basket was wrong until it was audited against the Accord Cadre, and the fix was one line.

The finding: the binding reference is rarely the market you are watching

Spain is cut 29%, €560 to €400, by a mandated CIPM decision. It is the only decision anyone takes. Here is what the six markets actually do.

Figure 1 — UC1 cascade. One decision, taken in Spain. Two markets move; the one you would expect does not.

Türkiye does not move — and that is the point

Spain sits directly in Türkiye's basket, on a MIN rule. Intuition says a 29% Spanish cut must hit Türkiye hard. It does nothing at all. Greece is priced at €310 — already below Spain's post-cut €400 — so Greece binds the Turkish floor both before and after. Spain never becomes the minimum, and Türkiye never recalculates.

The market with the most direct exposure to Spain on paper turns out to have none. Under a MIN rule only the binding reference matters, and it is rarely the market on the agenda. This is what a basket-aware model tells you and a spreadsheet of bilateral relationships does not.

Germany is not a cascade source. It is a cascade cliff. At €1,164 it sits nearly double the next market, so under a MIN rule it can never bind France or Spain — it would have to fall by more than half first. A German cut of €234 propagates nothing, and the model's golden tests assert exactly that. An AMNOG "no additional benefit" outcome forcing a steep cut is the one event that makes Germany the binding reference for several markets simultaneously — which is why manufacturers have withdrawn products from Germany rather than accept the cut and let it travel.1

France's price falls the same €160 as Spain's, but its three-year damage is smaller in percentage terms because the one-year lag spares it the first year. The Netherlands only feels year three. Volume is held constant — a mandated cut is a price event, not a demand event.

Downstream exposure exceeds the decision itself

Spain's own loss is €2.46M. France and the Netherlands together lose €2.52M — slightly more. A commercial team assessing the Spanish CIPM decision on its own terms sees less than half of what it actually costs, and the remainder lands in years two and three, after the budget cycle that approved it has closed.

That is the case for modelling the basket rather than the market.

Excluded — not modelled

Voluntary price reductions — commercially chosen, not regulator-imposed

Confidential rebates — invisible to baskets by construction

Price freezes & special agreements — suspend the mechanism

Political overrides — not reducible to rules

Excluding voluntary cuts is deliberate. A voluntary reduction is a lever a company may choose to pull; a mandated cut is an event it must absorb. Mixing the two makes the exposure figure unactionable.

The three use cases

UC1Mandatory price change cascade — post-launch, baskets fully populated. A regulator mandates a cut; the engine recalculates every market referencing it, over three years. The demo case, above.
UC2Launch sequence optimisation — pre-launch, baskets fill progressively. Launch order determines which prices are visible when each subsequent market sets its own. The sequence is the decision.
UC3HTA outcome stress test — post-launch. A restricted or negative HTA decision applies price erosion and volume restriction together, then propagates the price component onward.

Architecture

Figure 2 — Three UI layers over a deterministic, unit-tested Python core. Four golden tests guard the cascade logic.

Where the AI sits — and where it does not

The AI does

Read the completed cascade table and impact figures

Structure them as a C-suite memo — answer first, supporting logic after

Frame regulatory exposure in net sales and gross margin terms

The AI does not

Calculate the cascade — that is deterministic Python, unit-tested

Parse or infer IRP rules — they live in baskets.yaml, readable by anyone

Produce figures of its own — every number it cites comes from the engine

This split is the design decision I would defend hardest. Pricing logic that a regulator or a finance team might interrogate has to be reproducible and testable. The language model sits downstream of the maths, never inside it. Its output is advisory and requires expert human review before any commercial decision.

Limitations & roadmap

Synthetic data only — no real pricing conclusion follows from any output

Marginal transmission, not strict ceiling — the assumption that generates the cascade at all. See the engine section.

France is modelled as a ceiling, not a floor — correct basket, inverted direction. Over-predicts French erosion. See source 2.

Reference markets are held constant — Greece pins Türkiye's floor and never moves. A real Greek price would itself fall, so the model understates true cascade depth.

Lags are per market, applied after convergence — a market that would move twice across a real multi-round revision cycle is credited with one lagged move

Spain's basket is unverified — the MIN rule is sourced, the five-country composition is an assumption. Does not affect UC1, where Spain is the trigger. See source 4.

HTA thresholds are heuristics — qualitative and political factors excluded

Does not generalise across therapeutic areas for free — a different TA needs category-specific rules layered on this engine. "More data through the same engine" would be an overclaim.
